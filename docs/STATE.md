## What works today (user-visible)

- **Looks and the visual pass** (ADR-064): three looks (Clubroom, Studio, Night) picked on first open (and once by accounts set up before), changeable on Account (or Your data without accounts), applied before the first paint and carried with the account. Lessons are pages: reading pages with a small captioned figure and never "to move"; questions marked "Your turn" with a full board and a short lead-in, never scrolling. One button system ("Show the answer", "Done", back links naming the place), tab icons, Today as one card with the plan and Start training, Learn with "N of 29 learned", an Up next card and five plain labels, the concept page with one main action.
- **Accounts for invited friends** (ADR-063): the published app opens on an install guide on iPhone, iPad and Safari on the Mac ("Add Chess Trainer to your Home Screen", with "Use it in the browser instead"), then "Continue with Google"; elsewhere sign-in comes first and the browser's own install prompt is offered after it. Someone not on the list sees "Not on the list yet" and nothing else. An invited friend sees "Hi, Sam", links Chess.com (found by username, shown with name and ratings to confirm, or skipped) and lands on Today, which greets by name ("Good afternoon, Sam"); later opens go straight to Today, offline too. The initial in Today's corner opens Account (Chess.com link, whether progress is saved, how to install on a phone, sign out; for the admin Manage access and the private GitHub copy). Manage access (admin only): invite by name and Google address, see who signed in and whether they linked Chess.com, remove access, then delete a removed person's saved progress. Each account has its own data on a device and a copy in the account, merged on open and sent when practice changes; settings and removals travel. The list is enforced by Firestore rules; no address is in the source. Flags, the GitHub copy, the Chessreps courses and the starting ratings are the admin's; the admin can flag any screen with a note. A build without a Firebase config (development, tests) runs with no accounts, as before.
- **One layout for every exercise** (ADR-051): a bar at the top (the way out, the block and its clock or a count such as "6 left" or "Check 2 of 5", skip, flip), the board under it at full width and always in the same place, what is asked in one line under the board with the verdict taking that same place, and the buttons pinned to the bottom with the main one ("Next", "Try again", "Continue") always in the same spot. Puzzles, "Your move", own-game questions, missed moves, the pawn ending and opening recall all use it; lessons use the same pinned Continue / Back; a lesson or check opened from a concept's page has the same frame. A right answer rings the board green for a moment, a wrong one shakes it red; all motion is off under the reduced-motion setting.
- **The result as moves on one board** (ADR-052): after an answer on a position from your own games the moves in play are chips under the board, always in the order You, Game, Best and always in the same colours (amber, slate, blue), one chip for two that are the same move ("You · Game"). The result rests on the position that was asked, with an arrow for each chip. Tapping a chip plays that line to where it is decided; tapping it again returns. Under each move one bar says how good the position is for you after it, fuller is better, with the engine's evaluation in pawns at its end ("+0.11"; plus is good for you, whichever colour you have; ADR-053). The rating is a word and a mark ("Blunder." ??), and the mark sits on your piece while your move is on the board; a piece of yours that is really hanging is ringed there, with a thin arrow from what takes it. "Your move", the missed-move drill, "is this move safe?", "anything to win?" and a puzzle once it is solved or its solution shown all use it (ADR-054).
- **Nothing before, everything after** (ADR-054): before the answer an exercise says whose move it is and no more ("White to move"); a puzzle does not name its concept, and a position from your games does not say that it is one. The own-game questions keep their question and lose the game. After the answer the source stands small under the verdict ("Your daily game, Oct 12, move 10.", or a puzzle's themes and rating); tapping a puzzle's line opens each theme in Lichess's words and the link to the puzzle.
- **The engine's other choices** (ADR-054): under the chips, while the result rests on the position asked, up to three more moves with their numbers ("About as good: Bc1 +0.36, a4 +0.32, Qe3 +0.25"), each of which plays its line when tapped, in violet. The row is headed "About as good", "Much worse" or "Others" by how they stand against the first choice of the same search. With a line on the board its moves take the row's place. Puzzles carry numbers too: a wrong move says what it leaves and what the best move gives, a shown solution stands beside the wrong move as two chips with numbers, a solved puzzle shows "You · Solution" with its number.
- **Trying a move on the result** (ADR-055): once a position is answered a piece can be moved on the board wherever it stands, at the position asked or any number of moves into any line, for whichever side is to move. A move that is not already on the screen becomes a fourth chip, "Try", in violet, with the same bar and number as the others (how good the position is for you after it, also when the move tried was the opponent's); the engine's reply is played on the board at once and its line is written under the chips to step through. It plays on to where the line is decided and says the fact there, as every line does; otherwise it rests on the reply. A move made in a tried line tries again from there, so a position can be played out against the engine; moves made by hand are marked in the written line. A move tried later than the position asked is named with its number ("Try 11."). Making the first move of a line already shown opens that line; making the move a line goes on with steps on. Tapping the chip returns to the position asked and the try stays until another replaces it. In "Your move", the missed-move drill, both own-game questions and a puzzle once solved or shown; not while a puzzle is still being asked. Nothing is recorded for a try.
- **Review asks what was missed** (ADR-056): a position from your own games that is due is found by its game and move, whichever exercise missed it, and asked in the form it was last answered in; if that question no longer exists it is asked as "Your move". The card "This review position is no longer in the installed content pack" is gone: what really is not on the device any more is skipped and leaves the queue.
- **Going back** (ADR-052): the moves of the line on the board are written under the chips, each one a button, with ‹ and › either side; one step back from the first move is the position asked. A solved puzzle keeps its moves, so they can be gone back through. A control in the bar (↩) puts the previous position back exactly as it was left, lines and all, in the daily session's practice and test blocks, review, "Just practise" and the practice runs; its main button returns to where you were and nothing is recorded twice.
- **Today**: three buttons of one size in fixed places, "Start training · 30 min", "Play day · one rapid game", "Just practise" (ADR-045, ADR-051), with the reason for the study/play suggestion as one small line under them; the plan (four numbered blocks: review 4, learn 6, practice 16, test 4, with the reason for each), the other lengths (10/20/45/60) and the optional baseline are folded under one line; while a session runs the plan shows each block and its minutes without the reasons; in a session the lesson stored on the device opens at once; the practice block is led by "Your move"; one-tap start and resume; a clock per block; review hard-capped; new learning always reserved when a concept is ready; summary built from recorded attempts. A session in use at midnight keeps running. Ending the day asks once. Games actually played (fetched from Chess.com) count as play days.
- **Games and ratings arrive by themselves** (ADR-026): with a Chess.com username set (the published build carries the owner's, so the phone needs no setup), the app reads the public ratings and game archives when it is opened or brought to the front (at most every three hours, or on "Check Chess.com now"), and analyses new games on the device with a built-in engine while the app is visible. Nothing is sent anywhere else. Offline, rate-limited, unknown-player and error states are shown; the last good data stays.
- **Progress** (ADR-061): one page of cards, no folds and no period picker. *Next up*: one action by a fixed rule (the first moment of the latest analysed rated rapid game, else positions from his games due, else today's training). *Recent games*: the last three rated rapid games (result, colour, date, rating and change, big mistakes, an early blunder's move), each opening a game breakdown, and all games one row away. *In your games*: early blunders (last 20 games against the 20 before as squares, the measure fixed in ADR-027/032), rapid rating (a line over game number), big mistakes per 100 moves (the rate over each run of 20 games with a band clustered by game), chances taken (clear chances, one mark each), how you play (big mistakes by move number). *In training*, first looks only: spotting danger and spotting chances (spotted and false alarms side by side), your move (share sound, blocks of 20), fresh puzzles (share solved, blocks of 20). *Learning*: concept checks as a grid of 29 cells by state, practice as positions per day for 28 days. *More*: all time controls, retention, king-and-pawn endings, openings, play days, puzzle level, reading the ranges, your data and backup. A "Training" tick marks the first game since training began. Every card opens a screen with Recent / Trend / History windows of its own, the positions behind it and "About this measure" in a sentence or two.
- **Deep dive** (ADR-062): on Progress, three cards (Openings, Tactics, Strategy) open three pages, each with a filter row (rated rapid or all games; on Openings the colour too) kept in the address. *Openings*: an icicle of his first three moves each, coloured by his score, rare moves folded into "+n", a tapped move giving its line, results, name and games; openings by name with won/drawn/lost bars, each opening its games (names from the Lichess `chess-openings` dataset, CC0, a precached pack); one dot per game for his standing after move 10. *Tactics*: each tactic (hanging piece, fork, pin or skewer, discovered attack, mate, other; named on the board, `src/games/motifs.ts`) against him and for him, each opening its positions and its concept; a board of where his pieces fell with the five kinds of piece counted; puzzle tactics (five lowest, all on a tap) and puzzle difficulty by rating. *Strategy*: his last 20 games as rows of colour move by move with the move where they turn; winning positions won and lost positions saved; when he castled; the clock left, his against his opponents'; big mistakes by thinking time; how games ended. Each page ends with one sentence per card on what is counted.
- **Questions from your own games** (ADR-028/032): "is this move safe?" and "is there something here?", half of them controls where the honest answer is no. A position is asked as "nothing here" only where the game's move lost under ten points (ADR-062); answers to positions asked under the earlier rule are left out of Progress. In the practice block they alternate with new positions on the day's concept; in the test block they alternate with mixed puzzles and nothing is announced; they are also under Practice. Hits and false alarms are reported separately.
- **Just practise** (Today, and the top of Practice; ADR-037): one button, no plan. The app mixes what is due again, questions from your own games, your own missed moves and unseen positions on the least-tested concept, without saying which is coming, for as long as you like.
- **Your move** (Practice, and a quarter of "Just practise"; ADR-039): an ordinary position from your own games at moves 10 to 19. You play your move, then the reply you had to reckon with; the app rates your move against the engine's first choice (best, good, inaccuracy, mistake, blunder) and sets it beside the move from the game and the best move, each with what follows it (ADR-052); what of yours could already be taken, what you could have taken for nothing and what can be taken now are still worked out exactly and counted in Progress, and a piece that is really hanging is ringed on the board. Mistakes and blunders are recorded as misses (ADR-043). Beside a move rated good the engine's first choice is labelled "Also good", not "Best": the engine cannot tell moves within about 5 points apart (ADR-060). A position that was shown and left without an answer is owed and asked first next time; its answer is not a first look and is kept out of the timed comparison. About one position in three is played with 30 seconds for choosing the move, fixed by the position and not by how you are doing; the timed ones are announced (ADR-057): the board opens covered under "On the clock" for 1.3 seconds, the clock starts when the board appears and runs as a bar along the board's lower edge and a figure beside the question, red for the last ten seconds; running over never forces a move or changes its rating, the result says the flag fell; a streak of timed moves that were sound and in time is said on the announcement and under the rating, and a miss or a fallen flag ends it; Progress counts timed and untimed moves apart (ADR-044), from 17 November as a new series (`clock:2`), with the answers under the earlier quiet clock given apart.
- **The consequence on the board** (ADR-045, ADR-051, ADR-052): a line plays itself, one move every 650 ms, to where it is decided. After a wrong puzzle move the move stays on the board and the engine's answer is played at once, without being asked for; a shown solution plays through and must then be played by hand before the run goes on, in a check too, and so must the best move of an own missed move once it has been shown (ADR-058); on a position from your own games a line plays when its chip is tapped; a failed pawn ending can be tried again from the same start. Second goes are practice and never recorded. A replay stops where the line is decided and marks it (the piece that goes, the mate), with the fact in a few words ("Knight taken on e5. You end 2 points behind."); where that cannot be told, nothing is claimed.
- **Flag this one** (ADR-059): once a position is answered a flag stands in the run bar; one tap marks the position for a later look and keeps what the screen said, a second tap takes it off. Flags travel in the backup and reach the private copy; `scripts/flags.mjs` lists those not yet handled, and every session begins with them (`CLAUDE.md`).
- **Private copy for review** (ADR-048, off by default): with a private GitHub repository of the owner's and a token for it entered on the phone, the app puts its backup file there whenever practice data changes. `scripts/fetch-practice.sh` pulls it to this machine and `tools/audit` checks recent answers against a deep engine search (what the app scored and said against what it should have).
- **Results in a word** (ADR-050, then ADR-052): every result screen says right or not in a few words ("Mistake.", "Correct: O-O loses material.", "Kg7 is not the move."); what was better and by how much is shown by the moves and their bars. No scale explanations, no "recorded as", no sentences in notation.
- **How it went**: "Just practise" shows passed-of-scored and one line per kind of item at any time ("How is it going?") and at its end; the daily session's summary shows the same.
- **Baseline**: self-paced sampler; readings are only "untested", "provisional" or "pre-test to skip". The last position is always finished before the result screen appears.
- **Play days**: warm-up review, one 10+5 game on your own site, then a written reflection.
- **Learn**: 29 concepts in prerequisite order with stage and evidence, opening on one line of counts and a button to the concept the daily session would teach next (ADR-051). Sixteen lessons on the device, verbatim from Edward Lasker (1918) and Capablanca (1921), interactive and resumable, re-cut after a review that read them as a learner; the first seven concepts all have one, and lessons stored on the phone are taught before concepts that only have links, online or not. In a lesson example a sound move that is not the text's is not faulted and never recorded as a miss, and the text's move is still asked (ADR-036). Linked lessons on Lichess Practice, Chess.com and Chessreps with an explicit "I finished the lesson" step. Checks (5 unseen, no hints, 4 to pass, not skippable) are given in a later day's session, do not name their theme while running, and are asked again after 21 days; a stale or failed re-check is labelled (ADR-029). The check on a concept's page can still be taken at any time. Order (revision 4): piece safety, counting, threat scan, mate in one, opening principles, double attack, pin, defence first; pawn and rook endings and the Caro-Kann later.
- **Practice**: review queue (FSRS; due means due today) covering missed puzzles, lesson examples, repertoire moves, own-game positions and endgame starts; a position from your own games has one schedule whichever exercise asked it, comes back in the form it was last answered in, and is not served as new by another exercise (ADR-052, review policy v3); unseen positions by concept; own-game questions; tactics from Italian and Caro-Kann games; endgame positions; three-move calculation. After a wrong puzzle move the built-in engine's reply can be shown on request.
- **Exact endgames**: king and pawn against king against perfect play from 480 starts, judged by a complete table computed here. Underpromotion and a stalemating or hanging queen are scored as the draws they are.
- **Opening repertoire** (ADR-046): fills itself. The moves you play are read from your own games (played at least twice in a position), and the common positions you have no move for get a suggestion (the move most played at your rating among those within 3 points of the engine's first choice), each marked with where it came from; a move you record yourself replaces either, a move you remove stays removed, and in recall a different sound move can be made yours with one tap. Nothing is read from Chessreps: its terms forbid it, and the page says so. Inside the openings his courses cover (ADR-047) the course decides: the app suggests nothing there, asks him to confirm a move inferred from his games before it is recalled, points unanswered positions to the course, and lists games that left a confirmed move with a link back to the course. You can still record the move you play in each position, most frequently reached first, weighted by real Lichess move frequencies in a chosen rating band; engine evidence per move; recall on the review schedule (a changed move starts as a new item).
- **Own games** (Progress → More → All time controls): rating per time control (rated games only); major errors per 100 decisions with range and engine setting; missed chances led by those with one clearly best move, each counted once; errors when short of time, compared only on undecided positions; every number opens its positions; missed moves with one clearly best answer become drills.
- **Game breakdown and How you play** (ADR-061): a game's page shows the result, the rating after, big mistakes in so many moves, chances taken, the expected score before each of his moves with the moments marked, and the moments (first early blunder, largest big mistake, first clear chance missed, first big mistake short of time), each opening its position. How you play gives big mistakes by move number, by what the move did, by clock (under 10% of the base, undecided positions) and by colour, over the last 40 rated rapid games or all, each row opening its positions. The older cards are under More with their numbers unchanged.
- **Offline (the subway case)**: after one online visit the app shell, lessons, 8,219 puzzles, the opening book and the engine are on the device. With no network a whole session works: stored games and their numbers, on-device lessons, own-game questions with feedback, puzzles, the engine's reply to a wrong move, review, backup. Only two things need a connection: fetching new games (asking for a refresh says plainly that it is offline) and opening a linked lesson.

## What does not exist yet (do not describe as done)

- **Accounts, unverified parts** (ADR-063): no friend has signed in yet; sign-in inside the installed iPhone app (redirect through the self-hosted helper) has not been tried, nor the owner's phone adopting its database and sending the first copy. Not live sync: two devices open at once converge at the next open. The visual pass of the whole app has not started.
- **Nothing has been tested on the physical iPhone**: Home Screen install, touch on the board, backup to Files, and the first real fetch and on-device analysis of the owner's games (about 90 games; expect well over half an hour of the app being open in total, resumable). On the iPhone: open https://kyranstar.github.io/chess-trainer/ in Safari, Share → Add to Home Screen, open it once online, then Progress → Your data → Check this device.
- **The headline comparison needs 40 measured rated rapid games** before it shows "last 20 against the 20 before"; until then it shows the overall share and how many games are waiting.
- **"Your move" is new**: Progress counts it (moves passed, timed against untimed, clock use in real games), but there is no optional revision step, no bank of time across several moves, and it is not yet used as the check for the piece-safety concepts. Checks that test an operation itself (the net of an exchange; is there a check, capture or mate) are not built.
- **Time pressure**: the outside reviewer's answer replaced the first design (ADR-044). What exists is a way to find out whether a clock makes the owner's moves worse (30 seconds on a third of positions, counted apart), not yet a training method shown to work. Since ADR-057 the timed third is announced, runs in sight and has a streak at stake; that this carries over to games is an inference, and the comparison starts again from 17 November. Not built: a run of several positions on one clock where the flag ends the run. A position shown is recorded as shown at once, so a reload cannot bring it back as a first look; answers given after the app left the screen are kept out of the comparison; the difference is shown with its range. Open from that review (the first item was closed by ADR-060: a position left by a reload is asked again): no games are set aside for assessment; no time-bank exercise; no measure of the clock at moves 10, 20 and 30 of real games.
- **From the first practice audit, not done** (ADR-049): two look-alike positions from the same game can still be served in one sitting; a puzzle whose first move was right is still recorded as a plain miss. The private copy is switched on on the owner's phone; the audit is run on request (`scripts/fetch-practice.sh`, then the audit command below).
- **Chessreps lines are not imported** (ADR-046): the owner asked for it; the site's terms forbid copying, automated collection, and use in another product. Open: the owner can ask Chessreps for an export of his own learned lines; with permission they can be imported through the existing PGN import.
- **From the second practice audit, not done** (ADR-052): no list of recent answers; "Last" goes back one position only and not across a block change, and opening recall does not have it; one borderline "anything to win?" label (a pawn on a deep search, asked as "nothing") was left.
- **From the UX review, not done** (ADR-045): after a "nothing here" verdict a suspected line is shown only if a move was played for it (the move's line is then one of the chips, ADR-052); lesson steps are not marked Read / Watch / Your turn and the board still turns with the side to move; a check does not record its first decision apart from the rest of the line; the pawn ending is drilled before it is taught; an ended session cannot be resumed. (The development note on concept pages without a check is gone, ADR-051.) Nothing is waiting in ChatGPT: all three reviews (first principles, time pressure, UX) came back and were acted on (ADR-042, ADR-044, ADR-045).
- Exact endgames cover king and pawn against king only. No conversion play-outs from won positions. No Lichess-study import. No sync (dropped, ADR-034); a backup file is the only copy outside the phone.
- 13 of 29 concepts have an on-device lesson (two of the lessons, on the passed pawn and on exchanging when ahead, now only show the book's line and ask nothing); the rest only have links, which cannot be opened underground (the plan says so and teaches the next on-device lesson instead); 2 have no lesson at all; 7 have no independent check. ## Known issues and risks

- From ADR-064's final review, deferred: lessons are still in the books' notation (the converter was reverted; see ADR-064 for how to redo it); white text on the dark looks' red and green (error strip, danger button, result marks) is under 4.5:1; board coordinates are about 2:1 in Studio and Night; "Check due" shows the day a lesson is studied although the check comes on a later day; a new device of an account that has not pulled its copy within six seconds asks for a look again; `src/learn/pages.ts` has untested holes no lesson hits (a lesson that is only boards and questions, three boards in a row); in lasker-pin a "From the text" position sits on the Diagram 32 page; on a play day the card's heading sits over the training plan while the filled button is below the card.
- From ADR-063's final review (fixed and deployed: opening on the kept answer without waiting on Firebase, a deleted copy sent again, the newer-app notice kept, removals and another player's games not merged back). Deferred, minor: Firestore network errors are shown as raw text, not "offline"; a restored device that has not practised may rewrite `copyAt` every two minutes; a failure opening the account's database leaves an endless splash; repertoire or journal edits that keep row counts are sent only with the next other change; an answer recorded during a merge may wait for the next change to be sent; restoring an old file without stamps stamps the filled settings with the restore time; the admin is asked to link Chess.com on first open even with a name inferred; an update activating on an offline launch needs a connection once; the copy is one transaction (Firestore's 10 MiB request cap, about 11 chunks). `openings.declined` merges last-writer-wins (a re-entered move must be able to leave it), so two devices removing different moves at the same time can lose one removal.
- From ADR-063: the old live smoke tests (`data/live/live-0xx`) assume no sign-in and fail now; only `live-063` applies to the published site, and practice flows are checked locally (local mode and the stand-in account service). The sign-in helper files must stay in step with Firebase's: the weekly workflow in `kyranstar/kyranstar.github.io` does it; if it ever fails, sign-in may break in some browsers. The Firebase project owner (the owner) can read any account's copy in the console; friends are told so on the welcome screen. A Chess.com name changed on two devices at once: the later change wins at the next merge. Input fields on the admin page are thin (default styling): the visual pass.
- From ADR-062: the motif names are a mechanical reading of the board (first move of the refutation or best line; a hanging piece within three moves); a tactic whose point comes later (a quiet move, a deflection, a trapped piece) is "Other", and a first move that happens to line up a pawn and a king can read as a pin. The deep dive reads `analysis` for events and `measure` for clocks and standings, as the older cards do. Opening names cover only what the CC0 dataset names; a game that leaves named positions early takes the last name it reached. The pages were looked at by the agent on a local copy of the owner's backup, not by the owner, and not on the physical phone. The names pack adds 0.42 MB (63 kB compressed) to the offline cache.
- From ADR-055: each move tried on a result costs one engine search on the phone (40,000 nodes; 60,000 in "Your move"), queued behind any search already running; with no answer in eight seconds the try has no number and no continuation. Only the last try is kept. The number on a try comes from its own search and can differ a little from a chip's for the same move. Nothing on the screen says that the board can be moved on. The tap guard (ADR-036) does not cover the board: a tap landing on it right after an answer selects a piece and does nothing else. The sentence under a decided line counts material along the engine's line (ADR-045); since ADR-062 the count ("You end N points ahead") is left out where the line's number points the other way, and only the capture is said. A compared line still ends where it is decided (ADR-045); going on from there is done by making a move.

- From ADR-054: from 15 November "Your move" and the missed-move drill no longer say what kind of position is asked, so answers before and after that date were given under different instructions (timed against untimed, fresh positions). Each position now costs one more engine search on the phone (four lines, 150,000 nodes), begun when it appears; the row of other moves is absent if the engine does not answer in eight seconds. The row's numbers come from a different search than the chips' and can disagree with them by a little. No sentence says why a move is good: only numbers, other moves, the result of a line and Lichess's theme descriptions (see ADR-054 for what was considered). The rating bands were not changed: a move a tenth of a pawn from the best can still be called an inaccuracy on the phone's small search.

- From the simplification round (ADR-051): none of the new layout or motion has been seen on the physical phone. (The long list under "All the numbers" is gone with ADR-061.)
- From ADR-061: the Progress charts were looked at by the agent on a local copy of the owner's backup at 390 px, light and dark, not by the owner. Charts on the overview are pictures (the card is the link); values are tapped on the measure's screen. 41 of his 45 rated rapid games predate training, so the game charts mostly show the baseline. The fixed generic "cue for the next game" Astra proposed was not built (no LLM-written teaching text). Days are local to the phone's time zone at the time of reading, not of the answer. (The long verdicts that ran under the pinned buttons and the notation in the own-game question's verdict are gone with ADR-052; the "Your move" result with a line open and its note is tested not to scroll at 390 by 664.)
- From ADR-053: the build of `696a977`, live from about 14:17 to the next deploy on 12 November, could say "You end N points ahead" (or behind) under a line on the strength of its first six moves when the engine's whole line gave the material back; nothing was recorded or scored from that sentence. My guess, not established: the tap fell in the 400 ms in which the tap guard (ADR-036) disables buttons after the puzzle under it changes phase.

The bar under a move is the engine's expected score, and the number at its end the same thing in pawns from the learner's side (ADR-053), not from White's as chess sites print it. The control for the last position is an icon (↩) with no word. Each "Your move" position now costs one more engine search (the reply to the game's move), started while the move is being chosen.

- Time in a block is counted only while the page is visible.
- The bundle is one script, deliberately unsplit so the whole app precaches: 1.13 MB (293 kB gzip) on 18 November; the build of 12 November was 1.07 MB, so the "about 600 kB" written here before was out of date. ADR-061 adds a few tens of kB.
