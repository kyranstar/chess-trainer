## What works today (user-visible)

- **One layout for every exercise** (ADR-051): a bar at the top (the way out, the block and its clock or a count such as "6 left" or "Check 2 of 5", skip, flip), the board under it at full width and always in the same place, what is asked in one line under the board with the verdict taking that same place, and the buttons pinned to the bottom with the main one ("Next", "Try again", "Continue") always in the same spot. Puzzles, "Your move", own-game questions, missed moves, the pawn ending and opening recall all use it; lessons use the same pinned Continue / Back; a lesson or check opened from a concept's page has the same frame. A right answer rings the board green for a moment, a wrong one shakes it red; all motion is off under the reduced-motion setting.
- **The result as moves on one board** (ADR-052): after an answer on a position from your own games the moves in play are chips under the board, always in the order You, Game, Best and always in the same colours (amber, slate, blue), one chip for two that are the same move ("You · Game"). The result rests on the position that was asked, with an arrow for each chip. Tapping a chip plays that line to where it is decided; tapping it again returns. Under each move one bar says how good the position is for you after it: fuller is better, no number. The rating is a word and a mark ("Blunder." ??), and the mark sits on your piece while your move is on the board; a piece of yours that is really hanging is ringed there, with a thin arrow from what takes it. "Your move", the missed-move drill, "is this move safe?", "anything to win?" and a puzzle's solution after a wrong move all use it.
- **Going back** (ADR-052): the moves of the line on the board are written under the chips, each one a button, with ‹ and › either side; one step back from the first move is the position asked. A solved puzzle keeps its moves, so they can be gone back through. A control in the bar (↩) puts the previous position back exactly as it was left, lines and all, in the daily session's practice and test blocks, review, "Just practise" and the practice runs; its main button returns to where you were and nothing is recorded twice.
- **Today**: three buttons of one size in fixed places, "Start training · 30 min", "Play day · one rapid game", "Just practise" (ADR-045, ADR-051), with the reason for the study/play suggestion as one small line under them; the plan (four numbered blocks: review 4, learn 6, practice 16, test 4, with the reason for each), the other lengths (10/20/45/60) and the optional baseline are folded under one line; while a session runs the plan shows each block and its minutes without the reasons; in a session the lesson stored on the device opens at once; the practice block is led by "Your move"; one-tap start and resume; a clock per block; review hard-capped; new learning always reserved when a concept is ready; summary built from recorded attempts. A session in use at midnight keeps running. Ending the day asks once. Games actually played (fetched from Chess.com) count as play days.
- **Games and ratings arrive by themselves** (ADR-026): with a Chess.com username set (the published build carries the owner's, so the phone needs no setup), the app reads the public ratings and game archives when it is opened or brought to the front (at most every three hours, or on "Check Chess.com now"), and analyses new games on the device with a built-in engine while the app is visible. Nothing is sent anywhere else. Offline, rate-limited, unknown-player and error states are shown; the last good data stays.
- **"Is it working?"** (top of Progress, ADR-027/032): rapid rating; the share of rated rapid games with an avoidable loss of material or allowed mate in the first 20 moves (last 20 games against the 20 before, with counts, range, all such moves per decision, the engine setting, and games still waiting to be measured); and results on questions from your own games.
- **Questions from your own games** (ADR-028/032): "is this move safe?" and "is there something here?", half of them controls where the honest answer is no. In the practice block they alternate with new positions on the day's concept; in the test block they alternate with mixed puzzles and nothing is announced; they are also under Practice. Hits and false alarms are reported separately.
- **Just practise** (Today, and the top of Practice; ADR-037): one button, no plan. The app mixes what is due again, questions from your own games, your own missed moves and unseen positions on the least-tested concept, without saying which is coming, for as long as you like.
- **Your move** (Practice, and a quarter of "Just practise"; ADR-039): an ordinary position from your own games at moves 10 to 19. You play your move, then the reply you had to reckon with; the app rates your move against the engine's first choice (best, good, inaccuracy, mistake, blunder) and sets it beside the move from the game and the best move, each with what follows it (ADR-052); what of yours could already be taken, what you could have taken for nothing and what can be taken now are still worked out exactly and counted in Progress, and a piece that is really hanging is ringed on the board. Mistakes and blunders are recorded as misses (ADR-043). About one position in three is played with 30 seconds for choosing the move, fixed by the position and not by how you are doing; running over never forces a move or changes its rating, it is noted as late; Progress counts timed and untimed moves apart (ADR-044).
- **The consequence on the board** (ADR-045, ADR-051, ADR-052): a line plays itself, one move every 650 ms, to where it is decided. After a wrong puzzle move the move stays on the board and the engine's answer is played at once, without being asked for; a shown solution plays through and can then be played by hand; on a position from your own games a line plays when its chip is tapped; a failed pawn ending can be tried again from the same start. Second goes are practice and never recorded. A replay stops where the line is decided and marks it (the piece that goes, the mate), with the fact in a few words ("Knight taken on e5. You end 2 points behind."); where that cannot be told, nothing is claimed.
- **Private copy for review** (ADR-048, off by default): with a private GitHub repository of the owner's and a token for it entered on the phone, the app puts its backup file there whenever practice data changes. `scripts/fetch-practice.sh` pulls it to this machine and `tools/audit` checks recent answers against a deep engine search (what the app scored and said against what it should have).
- **Results in a word** (ADR-050, then ADR-052): every result screen says right or not in a few words ("Mistake.", "Correct: O-O loses material.", "Kg7 is not the move."); what was better and by how much is shown by the moves and their bars. No scale explanations, no "recorded as", no sentences in notation.
- **How it went**: "Just practise" shows passed-of-scored and one line per kind of item at any time ("How is it going?") and at its end; the daily session's summary shows the same.
- **Baseline**: self-paced sampler; readings are only "untested", "provisional" or "pre-test to skip". The last position is always finished before the result screen appears.
- **Play days**: warm-up review, one 10+5 game on your own site, then a written reflection.
- **Learn**: 29 concepts in prerequisite order with stage and evidence, opening on one line of counts and a button to the concept the daily session would teach next (ADR-051). Sixteen lessons on the device, verbatim from Edward Lasker (1918) and Capablanca (1921), interactive and resumable, re-cut after a review that read them as a learner; the first seven concepts all have one, and lessons stored on the phone are taught before concepts that only have links, online or not. In a lesson example a sound move that is not the text's is not faulted and never recorded as a miss, and the text's move is still asked (ADR-036). Linked lessons on Lichess Practice, Chess.com and Chessreps with an explicit "I finished the lesson" step. Checks (5 unseen, no hints, 4 to pass, not skippable) are given in a later day's session, do not name their theme while running, and are asked again after 21 days; a stale or failed re-check is labelled (ADR-029). The check on a concept's page can still be taken at any time. Order (revision 4): piece safety, counting, threat scan, mate in one, opening principles, double attack, pin, defence first; pawn and rook endings and the Caro-Kann later.
- **Practice**: review queue (FSRS; due means due today) covering missed puzzles, lesson examples, repertoire moves, own-game positions and endgame starts; a position from your own games has one schedule whichever exercise asked it, comes back in the form it was last answered in, and is not served as new by another exercise (ADR-052, review policy v3); unseen positions by concept; own-game questions; tactics from Italian and Caro-Kann games; endgame positions; three-move calculation. After a wrong puzzle move the built-in engine's reply can be shown on request.
- **Exact endgames**: king and pawn against king against perfect play from 480 starts, judged by a complete table computed here. Underpromotion and a stalemating or hanging queen are scored as the draws they are.
- **Opening repertoire** (ADR-046): fills itself. The moves you play are read from your own games (played at least twice in a position), and the common positions you have no move for get a suggestion (the move most played at your rating among those within 3 points of the engine's first choice), each marked with where it came from; a move you record yourself replaces either, a move you remove stays removed, and in recall a different sound move can be made yours with one tap. Nothing is read from Chessreps: its terms forbid it, and the page says so. Inside the openings his courses cover (ADR-047) the course decides: the app suggests nothing there, asks him to confirm a move inferred from his games before it is recalled, points unanswered positions to the course, and lists games that left a confirmed move with a link back to the course. You can still record the move you play in each position, most frequently reached first, weighted by real Lichess move frequencies in a chosen rating band; engine evidence per move; recall on the review schedule (a changed move starts as a new item).
- **Own games** (Progress): rating per time control (rated games only); major errors per 100 decisions with range and engine setting; missed chances led by those with one clearly best move, each counted once; errors when short of time, compared only on undecided positions; every number opens its positions; missed moves with one clearly best answer become drills.
- **Progress**: opens on the "Is it working?" headline (rating, early blunders, fresh positions, each with its count and range; how they are measured is folded under one line). Everything else is under "All the numbers", and backup and restore under "Your data and backup", each remembered once opened (ADR-045, ADR-051). There: unseen puzzle accuracy with n and range, per-concept table with "untested", retention by gap with the scheduler's forecast, exact endgame technique, curriculum coverage, opening preparedness, logged games, practice-difficulty estimate, backup/restore (now including preferences), storage-protection status, installed content and licences.
- **Offline (the subway case)**: after one online visit the app shell, lessons, 8,219 puzzles, the opening book and the engine are on the device. With no network a whole session works: stored games and their numbers, on-device lessons, own-game questions with feedback, puzzles, the engine's reply to a wrong move, review, backup. Only two things need a connection: fetching new games (asking for a refresh says plainly that it is offline) and opening a linked lesson.

## What does not exist yet (do not describe as done)

- **Nothing has been tested on the physical iPhone**: Home Screen install, touch on the board, backup to Files, and the first real fetch and on-device analysis of the owner's games (about 90 games; expect well over half an hour of the app being open in total, resumable). On the iPhone: open https://kyranstar.github.io/chess-trainer/ in Safari, Share → Add to Home Screen, open it once online, then Progress → Your data → Check this device.
- **The headline comparison needs 40 measured rated rapid games** before it shows "last 20 against the 20 before"; until then it shows the overall share and how many games are waiting.
- **"Your move" is new**: Progress counts it (moves passed, timed against untimed, clock use in real games), but there is no optional revision step, no bank of time across several moves, and it is not yet used as the check for the piece-safety concepts. Checks that test an operation itself (the net of an exchange; is there a check, capture or mate) are not built.
- **Time pressure**: the outside reviewer's answer replaced the first design (ADR-044). What exists is a way to find out whether a clock makes the owner's moves worse (30 seconds on a third of positions, counted apart), not yet a training method shown to work. A position shown is recorded as shown at once, so a reload cannot bring it back as a first look; answers given after the app left the screen are kept out of the comparison; the difference is shown with its range. Open from that review: after a reload a different position is asked rather than the same one; no games are set aside for assessment; no time-bank exercise; no measure of the clock at moves 10, 20 and 30 of real games.
- **From the first practice audit, not done** (ADR-049): two look-alike positions from the same game can still be served in one sitting; one own-game question could not be rebuilt from the games and was not investigated; a puzzle whose first move was right is still recorded as a plain miss. The private copy is switched on on the owner's phone; the audit is run on request (`scripts/fetch-practice.sh`, then the audit command below).
- **Chessreps lines are not imported** (ADR-046): the owner asked for it; the site's terms forbid copying, automated collection, and use in another product. Open: the owner can ask Chessreps for an export of his own learned lines; with permission they can be imported through the existing PGN import.
- **From the second practice audit, not done** (ADR-052): no list of recent answers; "Last" goes back one position only and not across a block change, and opening recall does not have it; one borderline "anything to win?" label (a pawn on a deep search, asked as "nothing") was left.
- **From the UX review, not done** (ADR-045): after a "nothing here" verdict a suspected line is shown only if a move was played for it (the move's line is then one of the chips, ADR-052); lesson steps are not marked Read / Watch / Your turn and the board still turns with the side to move; a check does not record its first decision apart from the rest of the line; the pawn ending is drilled before it is taught; an ended session cannot be resumed. (The development note on concept pages without a check is gone, ADR-051.) Nothing is waiting in ChatGPT: all three reviews (first principles, time pressure, UX) came back and were acted on (ADR-042, ADR-044, ADR-045).
- Exact endgames cover king and pawn against king only. No conversion play-outs from won positions. No Lichess-study import. No sync (dropped, ADR-034); a backup file is the only copy outside the phone.
- 13 of 29 concepts have an on-device lesson (two of the lessons, on the passed pawn and on exchanging when ahead, now only show the book's line and ask nothing); the rest only have links, which cannot be opened underground (the plan says so and teaches the next on-device lesson instead); 2 have no lesson at all; 7 have no independent check. The Capablanca lessons are in descriptive notation as printed.
- Opening data is deep (14 plies) only after 1.e4 e5 and 1.e4 c6.
- React components have no unit tests; they are covered only by the browser tests. "Old backup file" tests recompute checksums with current code rather than using frozen files.
- Not addressed from the UX audit: a confirm-before-judging option for moves (squares are 37 px in a Safari tab, 45 px installed), the repertoire builder's scrolling (the page now opens on recall and the next position, with the rest folded, ADR-051), VoiceOver access to the board, small touch targets on back links and chips.

## Known issues and risks

- From the simplification round (ADR-051): the long list under "All the numbers" was not shortened; none of the new layout or motion has been seen on the physical phone. (The long verdicts that ran under the pinned buttons and the notation in the own-game question's verdict are gone with ADR-052; the "Your move" result with a line open and its note is tested not to scroll at 390 by 664.)
- From ADR-052: the first phone open after this update rebuilds the review queue under policy v3 (one item per own-game position), by itself. The bar under a move is the engine's expected score with no number shown; in a position that is already bad every bar is short, which is true but can read as "all the same". The control for the last position is an icon (↩) with no word. Each "Your move" position now costs one more engine search (the reply to the game's move), started while the move is being chosen.

- The first phone open after this update measures all games again (analysis v4) and rebuilds the review queue under the new policy; both happen by themselves.
- Left open from the fourth-round reviews (ADR-036, last bullet): a reload in the practice block shows a different question; only the first lesson of a concept is offered by the session; the king-and-pawn check does not survive a reload; most concept checks measure general puzzle strength more than the lesson; a lesson's text and its diagram are on separate screens.
- Reports of the four reviews are not in the repository (they were written to a scratch folder); their reproductions that matter are now tests.

- A queued review item whose content is gone (retired puzzle, re-analysed game) shows a "no longer available" card and stays due.
- The first on-device measurement of a large backlog takes a long time on a phone and only runs while the app is visible.
- A sound sacrifice in a position that is already decided is counted as a target event (ADR-032).
- Chess.com's public data can lag by hours; "updates when opened" is subject to that.
- `SOUND_MARGIN` of 8 expected-score points is lenient near equality.
- Time in a block is counted only while the page is visible.
- The bundle is one script of about 600 kB (about 185 kB gzip), deliberately unsplit so the whole app precaches.
